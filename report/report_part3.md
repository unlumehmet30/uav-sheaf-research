**Missing versus wrong information is a real confusion in the simulator, and mostly a bookkeeping problem (E2–E4).** A test that treats stale reports as current keeps {{m:physics_naive:iid_0:pr_auc}} without loss but falls to {{m:physics_naive:burst_50:pr_auc}} under 50% bursts (Figure A2) and {{m:physics_naive:nodeburst_30:pr_auc}} under rx-burst loss; modelling the age of the report (AoI-aware physics) removes most of the damage ({{m:physics_aoi:burst_50:pr_auc}}, {{m:physics_aoi:nodeburst_30:pr_auc}}). **Other experiments.**

* *Noise (E2):* at ×3 noise all models lose accuracy (RA-SCM {{m:rascm:noise_3:pr_auc}}, NSD {{m:nsd:noise_3:pr_auc}}, GAT {{m:gat:noise_3:pr_auc}}) and the frozen thresholds fail (FPR {{m:rascm:noise_3:fpr}} for RA-SCM, {{m:gcn_drop:noise_3:fpr}} for GCN-drop; Table B1).
* *Anomaly type (E4):* sudden jumps are nearly always detected; drift and spoofing are harder (spoof at 30% loss: RA-SCM {{m:rascm:type_spoof_30:pr_auc}}, conventional GNNs {{m:gcn_drop:type_spoof_30:pr_auc}}–{{m:nsd:type_spoof_30:pr_auc}}) and detected later (Figure A8).
* *Targeted blackout (E4):* it hurts no learned model and helps those using per-link delivery state (from iid 30% on the same episodes: RA-SCM {{m:rascm:iid_30:pr_auc}} → {{m:rascm:targeted_30:pr_auc}}, GCN-drop {{m:gcn_drop:iid_30:pr_auc}} → {{m:gcn_drop:targeted_30:pr_auc}}), while the naive test falls ({{m:physics_naive:iid_30:pr_auc}} → {{m:physics_naive:targeted_30:pr_auc}}). The extra loss coincides exactly with the anomaly and a third of training episodes contain it, so missing packets can themselves become anomaly evidence (fewer neighbours receiving the corrupted reports is another, untested explanation); the condition overstates detectability against an attacker who does not degrade its own links.
* *Generalisation (E5, Figure A6):* trained on 10 UAVs, learned models transfer to 20 UAVs (RA-SCM {{m:rascm:gen_n20:pr_auc}}), an unseen trajectory ({{m:rascm:gen_lissajous:pr_auc}}) and formation ({{m:rascm:gen_grid:pr_auc}}), but **not to 5 UAVs** (RA-SCM {{m:rascm:gen_n5:pr_auc}}, GAT {{m:gat:gen_n5:pr_auc}}, GCN {{m:gcn:gen_n5:pr_auc}}), where few neighbours give little evidence.
* *Frozen-threshold metrics* (Table B1) differ partly because realised FPRs differ; the AoI-aware physics test is poorly calibrated by the validation rule (FPR {{m:physics_aoi:iid_0:fpr}} at 0% loss, target 0.05). *Cost* (Table B2, Figure A9): RA-SCM needs {{n:lat_rascm10}} ms per step at N = 10 versus {{n:lat_gat10}} ms for GAT, and {{n:rascm_train_min}} min of training per seed.

*Table 5. Ablations (E6, also Figure A4): paired PR-AUC difference full RA-SCM − ablated variant (mean [95% CI]); positive = the removed component helped.*

**Reading the ablations (exploratory beyond the MPNN-E clause).** The reliability *channel* (delivery state, age, link quality, age-compensated residual) matters most: removing it costs {{da:burst_50:rascm_no_rel}} under 50% bursts, leaving RA-SCM below all four conventional GNNs there ({{m:rascm_no_rel:burst_50:pr_auc}} vs {{m:gcn:burst_50:pr_auc}}–{{m:gcn_drop:burst_50:pr_auc}}), and {{da:nodeburst_30:rascm_no_rel}} under rx-burst loss. Learned *weights* matter only under 50% bursts ({{da:burst_50:rascm_no_weight}}, just above 0.02; ≈0 in the other Table 5 conditions). **Learned sheaf maps showed no measurable benefit:** identity maps change PR-AUC by at most {{n:sheaf_maxabs}}, with the interval including 0 in {{n:sheaf_ci0}} conditions; with 5 UAVs the identity variant has a higher mean ({{da:gen_n5:rascm_no_sheaf}}, interval includes 0) and MPNN-E is better than RA-SCM ({{da:gen_n5:mpnn_e}}). The GRU helps under bursts and at ×3 noise ({{m:rascm_no_temporal:noise_3:pr_auc}} without vs {{m:rascm:noise_3:pr_auc}}). MPNN-E is worse than RA-SCM under 50% bursts ({{da:burst_50:mpnn_e}}), more than either single removal, so the experiment cannot separate maps from weights. The benefit under loss is best attributed to *how reliability metadata enters the model* (edge features and, under bursts, learned weights, with temporal memory), not to sheaf structure.

**Threats to validity** (besides Section 7). (i) Realised FPRs differ across models. (ii) RA-SCM consumes edge features directly, the baselines in aggregated form; MPNN-E partly addresses this, but a GAT with edge features was not run. (iii) Learning rates were tuned on one seed. (iv) Targeted blackout rewards models that read delivery state; it is not a test against an adaptive attacker.

**Expected scientific impact.** The outcome is a *controlled benchmark and a negative-leaning answer*: reliability-aware sheaf modelling is at best a small improvement over strong graph baselines, and the cheapest effective step is to model the age of the evidence. Researchers gain loss models and an age-aware control for evaluating swarm detectors; designers gain evidence on thresholds under degraded links. For society, a detector that does not mistake outages for attacks would reduce needless aborts of inspection and search-and-rescue swarms and make multi-UAV safety cases more credible. *Possible harms:* an adversary could pair spoofing with more selective link degradation than tested here, and a benchmark can encourage over-trust in simulated accuracy. *New questions:* threshold calibration under noise shift; detection with few neighbours; whether margins survive measured noise and loss traces; adaptive attackers; whether computed physical maps [@barbero2022connection] can replace learned ones.
