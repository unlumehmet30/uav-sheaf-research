<div class="abstract"><p><strong>Abstract.</strong> A UAV swarm must tell a wrong position report from a merely stale one, because packet loss and localisation faults both create inconsistencies between reported and measured relative positions. Cooperative localisation tolerates lost links and faulty measurements jointly, and learned swarm detectors have been simulated with packet loss, but in the literature we searched none compares detectors of UAV position anomalies under controlled, condition-stratified loss, burst loss and age of information against an age-aware physical-consistency baseline. We propose RA-SCM (Reliability-Aware Sheaf Consistency Model), a sheaf-diffusion detector whose messages are read from the time of the last delivered packet, with edge-conditioned restriction maps and reliability weights derived from delivery state, age and link quality. We hypothesise that it beats the best capacity-matched GCN, GAT or neural-sheaf baseline by at least 0.02 PR-AUC (area under the precision–recall curve) in four of six loss conditions. A simulator with sudden, drift and spoof-like anomalies, iid, Gilbert–Elliott and targeted communication failures, leakage tests and five seeds supplies the evidence; nothing is validated on real flights. RA-SCM has the highest mean PR-AUC in all seven main conditions ({{m:rascm:iid_50:pr_auc}} at 50% iid loss, {{m:rascm:burst_50:pr_auc}} at 50% bursts) and beats an age-aware physics test, but the hypothesis is not supported: its margin over the best conventional GNN reaches 0.02 only under 50% bursts ({{dh:burst_50:best_conventional}}, 95% CI) and is absent without loss. Ablations attribute the gain to the reliability inputs, not to learned sheaf maps. The contribution is a falsifiable benchmark and a negative-leaning answer.</p></div>
