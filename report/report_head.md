<div class="abstract"><p><strong>Abstract.</strong> A UAV swarm must tell a genuinely wrong position report from a merely stale one, because packet loss and localisation faults both create inconsistencies between reported and measured relative positions. In 20 verified sources we found no study that evaluates learned graph or sheaf-based position-anomaly detectors under explicitly modelled loss, burst loss and age of information against an age-aware physical-consistency baseline (search coverage is limited: IEEE Xplore and ACM DL were not queried natively). We propose RA-SCM, a sheaf-diffusion detector whose messages are read from the time of the last delivered packet, with edge-conditioned restriction maps, an affine translation term and reliability weights derived from delivery state, age and link quality, and we test whether it outperforms capacity-matched GCN, GAT and neural-sheaf baselines. A lightweight simulator with sudden, drift and spoof-like anomalies, iid, Gilbert–Elliott and targeted communication failures, leakage tests and five seeds supplies the evidence; nothing is validated on real flights. RA-SCM has the highest mean PR-AUC in most loss conditions ({{m:rascm:iid_50:pr_auc}} at 50% iid loss, {{m:rascm:burst_50:pr_auc}} at 50% bursts) and clearly beats an age-aware physics test, but under the pre-specified rule the hypothesis is not supported: its margin over the best conventional GNN reaches 0.02 only under 50% bursts ({{dh:burst_50:best_conventional}}) and is absent without loss. Ablations attribute the gain to the communication-reliability inputs rather than to learned sheaf maps. A consistency test that ignores staleness collapses to {{m:physics_naive:burst_50:pr_auc}} under bursts. The contribution is a falsifiable benchmark and a negative-leaning answer.</p></div>
